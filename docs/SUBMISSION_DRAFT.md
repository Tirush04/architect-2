## Why would a non-technical user pick your platform?
*(Someone who already uses Replit, Lovable or Emergent.)*

> Draft, rewrite in your own words:
>
> Lovable and Replit get you a good-looking app fast, but the moment you ask for a change you're debugging a
> black box. Architect 2.0 starts with a Blueprint in plain English (pages, data, and what each agent does and
> when) and builds nothing until you approve it. After that, every change comes with a "what changed" summary
> and a checkpoint you can restore in one click. You can click any part of the preview and say what you want
> different. Agents aren't bolted on: you see them as a simple flow (trigger → agent → tools → a human approves
> anything irreversible) and can test them before going live. And when your engineer needs to take over, they
> open the same project in the Pro lens. There's no export and no rebuild.

## Why would a technical user pick your platform?
*(Someone who already uses Claude Code, Codex or Cursor.)*

> Draft, rewrite in your own words:
>
> Coding agents are great inside a repo but have no product surface, and they treat agents as just more code to
> write. Architect 2.0 gives developers the repo loop they expect (file tree, editor, per-turn diffs, checkpoints,
> GitHub push and repo import, env vars, an API with keys) and makes agents first-class. You pick the framework
> (Lyzr ADK, LangGraph, CrewAI, OpenAI Agents SDK, Claude Agent SDK or AutoGen), switch it in one click, and get
> real code, not a proprietary config. The planning and UI scaffolding that take the first day in Cursor happen
> in minutes, and a business stakeholder can iterate on copy and flows in the Guided lens without filing tickets.
